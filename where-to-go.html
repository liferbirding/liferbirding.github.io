Where to go birding

The best patches, reserves and hotspots for finding birds.

Great birding starts with knowing where to look. Discover great birding spots, from wetlands and rainforests to coastlines, woodlands and city parks. Organised by country and region, so you can find your next birding adventure wherever you are.

We're building it with local venues, reserves and birding partners. Look for the gold gem for places offering Lifer Pro member perks.

Hunter

9 places

  • Barrington Tops National Park

    World Heritage-listed Gondwana rainforest park rising from near sea level to over 1,500 metres, taking in Antarctic beech forest and sub-alpine swamps. NPWS lists eleven separate birdwatching and wildlife-encounter sites across the park.

    Visit website

  • Blackbutt Reserve

    A City of Newcastle bushland reserve with free wildlife exhibits and a network of trails. Superb Lyrebirds and Eastern Whipbirds are heard along the tracks.

    Visit website

  • Hunter Region Botanic Gardens

    Extensive grevillea and native-plant collections that draw honeyeaters and Eastern Spinebills. There is a cafe and nursery on site.

    Visit website

  • Hunter Valley Wildlife Park

    Wildlife park at Nulkaba near Cessnock with a walk-through lorikeet enclosure where the birds fly free around visitors, set within a collection of over 200 Australian and exotic animals.

    Visit website

  • Hunter Wetlands Centre

    A Ramsar-listed wetland on the edge of Newcastle, with trails and a dedicated birdwatching area across the ponds and reedbeds. One of the best local sites for Magpie Geese, Freckled Duck and migratory waders.

    Visit website

  • Hunter Wetlands National Park

    Ramsar-listed estuary wetland on Newcastle's doorstep, holding the second-largest area of mangroves in New South Wales. NPWS names Stockton Sandspit as a leading spot for roosting and feeding shorebirds, with pelicans, cormorants and sea-eagles around Riverside Park, and more than 200 species recorded across the Hunter estuaries.

    Visit website

  • Oakvale Wildlife Park

    A hands-on, ZAA-accredited wildlife park near Port Stephens with free-ranging kangaroos and a walk-in aviary.

    Visit website

  • Tilligerry Habitat

    A volunteer-run koala reserve on Port Stephens with a foreshore boardwalk. Good for honeyeaters and White-bellied Sea-Eagles.

    Visit website

  • Worimi Conservation Lands

    Four thousand hectares of Stockton Bight beach, dune and swale country co-managed by Worimi Traditional Owners and NPWS, adjoining the Ramsar-listed Hunter estuary. 108 bird species recorded, with Pied Oystercatcher nesting in the swales and Pacific Golden-Plovers roosting behind the frontal dunes.

    Visit website

New South Wales

23 places

  • Australian Botanic Garden Mount Annan

    Australia's largest botanic garden, with free-ranging Emus and wetlands that draw honeyeaters and waterbirds. Visitor centre and cafe on site.

    Visit website

  • Australian Reptile Park

    Somersby wildlife park whose Eric's Nature Walk runs through bushland and includes an aviary of native Australian birds alongside a nocturnal house and frog pond.

    Visit website

  • Bicentennial Park (Sydney Olympic Park)

    Estuarine wetlands and parkland at Sydney Olympic Park, with over 200 bird species recorded. The wetlands host migratory shorebirds including Latham's Snipe.

    Visit website

  • Blue Mountains Botanic Garden Mount Tomah

    A cool-climate botanic garden at around 1,000 metres in the Blue Mountains. Gang-gang Cockatoos and Crimson Rosellas are regular, and there is a visitor centre and cafe.

    Visit website

  • Blue Mountains Heritage Centre

    The NPWS gateway to the Blue Mountains at Blackheath, with displays and a shop. Nearby trails hold Superb Lyrebirds, Gang-gang Cockatoos and the NSW-endemic Rockwarbler.

    Visit website

  • Booderee Botanic Gardens

    Australia's only Aboriginal-owned botanic garden, on Jervis Bay. The Jervis Bay area is a stronghold for the endangered Eastern Bristlebird.

    Visit website

  • Centennial Parklands

    Large inner-Sydney parkland of ponds, lakes, paperbark swamp, woodland and open grassland with around 130 recorded bird species. Nankeen Night Heron, Royal Spoonbill and Musk Duck work the water, with Tawny Frogmouth roosting near Lachlan Swamp and Pacific Baza overhead.

    Visit website

  • Cumberland State Forest

    Australia's only metropolitan state forest — forty hectares of tall eucalypt forest, moist rainforest gully and a 1930s arboretum at West Pennant Hills. Forestry Corporation names parrots and lorikeets throughout, with Eastern Whipbird and Satin Bowerbird as the sought-after species along the Forestry, Palm Gully and Sensory trails.

    Visit website

  • Deniliquin Visitor Information & Peppin Heritage Centre

    The Riverina visitor centre that serves as the base for Deniliquin's Plains-wanderer night tours. The centre sits in Deniliquin's CBD near the Edward River, on the town's first public school site, with a museum, gallery and the Warriston Ram Shed.

    Visit website

  • Dorrigo Rainforest Centre

    The NPWS centre for World Heritage Gondwana rainforest at Dorrigo, with the Rainforest Skywalk over the canopy. Regent and Satin Bowerbirds and Paradise Riflebirds occur here.

    Visit website

  • Eurobodalla Regional Botanic Garden

    Garden set within retained native bushland in Mogo State Forest on the far south coast, with walking trails through Naturescape and Sandstone gardens. The Garden names Superb Lyrebird among the species visitors may find and runs an iNaturalist project recording wildlife on site.

    Visit website

  • Featherdale Sydney Wildlife Park

    Doonside park holding a large collection of Australian native wildlife, with a walk-through lorikeet enclosure. Southern Cassowary, Little Penguin and Glossy Black-Cockatoo are among the birds profiled, along with the critically endangered Regent Honeyeater, which Featherdale breeds and releases to the wild.

    Visit website

  • Ku-ring-gai Wildflower Garden

    A hundred and twenty-three hectares of remnant Sydney sandstone bushland at St Ives, with heath, tall forest, gullies and ponds. Council runs its own bird walk here, with honeyeaters the dominant group and Glossy Black-Cockatoo among the drawcards.

    Visit website

  • North Coast Regional Botanic Garden

    Twenty hectares in central Coffs Harbour blending rainforest, mangroves along the Coffs Creek walk, paperbark forest, ponds and lakes. The Garden names Rufous Fantail, Rainbow Bee-eater, Sacred Kingfisher and Spectacled Monarch among its spring and summer visitors, and publishes birding leaflets.

    Visit website

  • Potoroo Palace Native Animal Sanctuary

    Non-profit native animal sanctuary on bush property at Yellow Pinch near Merimbula. Free-roaming Emus, ducks and geese move through the grounds, with cockatoos and other native birds alongside rescued marsupials.

    Visit website

  • Royal Botanic Garden Sydney

    Harbourside gardens in central Sydney with more than 140 recorded bird species. The resident Powerful Owls and Tawny Frogmouths are the best-known of them, and there is a Visitor Centre on site.

    Visit website

  • Sea Acres Rainforest Centre

    An elevated boardwalk through coastal littoral rainforest at Port Macquarie, good for Wonga Pigeons and Rufous Fantails. NPWS-run, with a cafe and shop.

    Visit website

  • Symbio Wildlife Park

    Landscaped wildlife park at Helensburgh south of Sydney, with individual exhibits for Emu and Wedge-tailed Eagle and a penguin colony among more than 500 animals.

    Visit website

  • Taronga Western Plains Zoo

    Open-range zoo on 300 hectares of Wiradjuri Country near Dubbo, with Emu among the Australian species on display and wetland and billabong areas along the circuit. Taronga runs Regent Honeyeater recovery breeding.

    Visit website

  • Taronga Zoo Sydney

    A harbourside zoo with a long-running free-flight bird show. Wild Powerful Owls also turn up in the grounds.

    Visit website

  • Wagga Wagga Botanic Gardens

    Free-entry council gardens on Willans Hill whose zoo and aviary precinct centres on a large walk-through free-flight aviary of roughly 100 birds — finches, wrens, doves, lorikeets, and Princess, Superb and King Parrots. Ducks, peafowl, Black Swans and geese roam the grounds freely.

    Visit website

  • Walgun Cape Byron Information Centre

    Clifftop headland reserve at Australia's most easterly point, mixing littoral rainforest, heathland and coastal cliffs. NPWS names White-bellied Sea-Eagle, Australian Pelican, Australian Brush-turkey and Superb Fairywren, with fruit-doves and cuckooshrikes moving through the coastal corridor.

    Visit website

  • Warrumbungle Visitor Centre

    The gateway to Warrumbungle National Park, Australia's first Dark Sky Park. Turquoise Parrots and Diamond Firetails are among the birds found here, and the centre has interpretive displays.

    Visit website

Victoria

8 places

  • Ballarat Wildlife Park

    Privately owned park on thirty-seven acres of natural bushland at Ballarat East. Free-roaming Emus wander the grounds with the kangaroo mob, and the collection includes cassowaries, a colony of Little Penguins, and raptor and parrot species.

    Visit website

  • Healesville Sanctuary

    A Zoos Victoria sanctuary, and home to the Helmeted Honeyeater breeding programme — the recovery centre for Victoria's critically endangered bird emblem.

    Visit website

  • Phillip Island Nature Parks

    Home of the Penguin Parade, where Little Penguins come ashore in front of visitors. Summer brings rafts of Short-tailed Shearwaters back to their burrows.

    Visit website

  • Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria — Cranbourne Gardens

    Australian-plant botanic garden at Cranbourne combining the designed Australian Garden with more than ten kilometres of tracks through remnant heathland and woodland. RBGV names Superb Fairywren, New Holland Honeyeater and Willie-wagtail among the birds of the Australian Garden.

    Visit website

  • Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria — Melbourne Gardens

    Thirty-eight hectares of lakes, lawns and mixed plantings on the edge of Melbourne's CBD. The Gardens name coots, cormorants, spinebills and swans among their resident birdlife, with waterbirds nesting around the Ornamental Lake.

    Visit website

  • Serendip Sanctuary

    A Parks Victoria sanctuary with long-running Brolga and Australian Bustard breeding, set among open wetlands full of waterbirds.

    Visit website

  • Werribee Open Range Zoo

    Open-range zoo on the banks of the Werribee River. The Australian Trail runs through bush and grassland where Emus range alongside kangaroos, and the zoo profiles the critically endangered Orange-bellied Parrot as a featured species.

    Visit website

  • Wilsons Promontory National Park

    Granite and heathland coastal park at mainland Australia's southern tip, with beaches, dunes, warm-temperate rainforest and grassy woodland. Parks Victoria states half of Victoria's bird species occur here, and names beach-nesting Hooded Plover, Ground Parrot and free-ranging Emus.

    Visit website

Queensland

8 places

  • Australia Zoo

    Large wildlife park at Beerwah with an extensive Australian and exotic bird collection, Southern Cassowary, Brolga, Black-necked Stork and Wedge-tailed Eagle among them. Free-flight birds feature in the Crocoseum conservation show arena.

    Visit website

  • Brisbane Botanic Gardens Mt Coot-tha

    Fifty-six hectares of subtropical botanic gardens with a lagoon, rainforest, eucalypt forest, heathland and wetland plantings across twenty-seven hectares of Australian plant communities. Council names egrets, cormorants and Dusky Moorhens on the lagoon.

    Visit website

  • Cairns Botanic Gardens

    Tropical rainforest gardens in Cairns, alive with Orange-footed Megapodes, Double-eyed Fig-Parrots and Metallic Starlings.

    Visit website

  • Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary

    A National Trust sanctuary on the Gold Coast, where the wild Rainbow Lorikeet feeding has been a birding spectacle for decades.

    Visit website

  • Daintree Discovery Centre

    An aerial walkway and canopy tower through the Daintree rainforest, in prime Southern Cassowary country.

    Visit website

  • David Fleay Wildlife Park

    Mangrove and melaleuca wetland park at Burleigh Heads with Australian animals in habitat-inspired enclosures. The bird collection includes Brolga, Black-necked Stork, Southern Cassowary, eagles and owls, alongside platypus and crocodiles.

    Visit website

  • Lone Pine Koala Sanctuary

    Riverside sanctuary at Fig Tree Pocket with a dedicated bird collection and a free-flight raptor arena flying eagles, owls, kites and falcons. Wild Rainbow Lorikeets come in to the sanctuary's open feeding area.

    Visit website

  • O'Reilly's Rainforest Retreat

    A long-established birding retreat in Lamington National Park. Regent Bowerbirds feed from the hand and Albert's Lyrebird calls through the surrounding rainforest.

    Visit website

South Australia

6 places

  • Adelaide Botanic Garden

    Fifty hectares in the city centre with lakes, the Nelumbo Pond, palm and rainforest glasshouses and mature plantings. The Garden's wildlife page names resident swans, ducks, ibis and kookaburras, and the adjoining Botanic Park holds a Grey-headed Flying-fox camp.

    Visit website

  • Adelaide Zoo

    Eight-hectare city zoo in botanic surrounds with a walk-through rainforest and wetlands aviary. Southern Cassowary, Superb Lyrebird and Palm Cockatoo are on display, alongside the critically endangered Orange-bellied Parrot and Regent Honeyeater.

    Visit website

  • Cleland Wildlife Park

    Open wildlife park in the Mount Lofty Ranges where most animals live in natural stringybark forest and wetland habitat rather than enclosures. The park describes native bush birds, forest birds and waterbirds as part of the visit.

    Visit website

  • Laratinga Wetlands

    Constructed wetland on the edge of Mount Barker, built as part of the wastewater reclamation scheme and now a chain of open ponds, reedbeds and revegetated banks linked by walking trails.

    Visit website

  • Urimbirra Wildlife Park

    Open-range park set in native wetlands on the Fleurieu Peninsula holding over 400 Australian native animals. Birds the park names include cockatoos, cassowary and owls, with Emu hand-feeding at the entrance.

    Visit website

  • Warrawong Wildlife Sanctuary

    Thirty-five-acre feral-proof sanctuary at Mylor in the Adelaide Hills, fenced since the 1960s against cats and foxes, with springs, streams, lakes and rainforest gully habitat. The sanctuary states that birds rarely seen outside the fence live protected inside it.

    Visit website

Western Australia

6 places

  • Caversham Wildlife Park

    Western Australia's largest privately owned native wildlife collection, inside Whiteman Park, with around 2,000 animals. Australian Bustard, Black-breasted Buzzard, Gang-gang Cockatoo and Wedge-tailed Eagle are among the birds it names, and there is a penguin feeding attraction.

    Visit website

  • Herdsman Lake Discovery Centre (Wildlife Centre)

    One of Perth's richest wetlands for crakes, rails and nesting waterbirds, minutes from the city. Run by the WA Gould League.

    Visit website

  • Kanyana Wildlife Rehabilitation Centre

    Volunteer-run wildlife sanctuary and hospital set among jarrah trees at Lesmurdie in the Perth hills. Resident birds include Eastern Barn Owl, Rainbow Lorikeet, Red-tailed Black-Cockatoo, Tawny Frogmouth and Emu, and the centre runs early morning bird walks.

    Visit website

  • Kings Park and Botanic Garden

    Bushland and botanic garden above the Swan River in Perth, where flocks of the endangered Carnaby's Black-Cockatoo come in to feed.

    Visit website

  • Perth Zoo

    Metropolitan zoo on the Swan River foreshore with a substantial native and exotic bird collection. Baudin's and Carnaby's Black-Cockatoo, Brolga, Bush Stone-curlew and Southern Cassowary are among the species it names.

    Visit website

  • The Wetlands Centre Cockburn

    Community wetlands centre at the heart of the Beeliar Wetlands beside Bibra Lake, with banksia woodland, bushland trails and a wildflower garden. Waterbird numbers on Bibra Lake rise through summer and autumn as smaller lakes dry; bush birds include honeyeaters and black-cockatoos.

    Visit website

Tasmania

4 places

  • Bonorong Wildlife Sanctuary

    Sanctuary and wildlife hospital at Brighton north of Hobart, combining resident-animal enclosures with a statewide rescue operation. Birds it names are Sulphur-crested Cockatoo, Yellow-tailed Black-Cockatoo, Tawny Frogmouth and Cape Barren Goose, and it runs a seabird rehabilitation programme.

    Visit website

  • Inala Nature Museum & Jurassic Garden

    On Bruny Island, the gateway to the endangered Forty-spotted Pardalote and all twelve Tasmanian endemics.

    Visit website

  • The Tasmanian Arboretum

    Sixty-six hectares of temperate-world trees at Eugenana south of Devonport, taking in open parkland, original forest, limestone outcrops, Founders Lake, a creek and a river. Around 81 bird species have been recorded on site, with Tasmanian Nativehen conspicuous, and the Arboretum maintains its own species list.

    Visit website

  • Trowunna Wildlife Sanctuary

    Eighty-five acres at Mole Creek where alpine, forest and coastal habitats meet, around a central lake. The lake supports ducks, geese and swans; the forest holds goshawks, falcons, honeyeaters, wrens and rosellas, and a resident Wedge-tailed Eagle has lived at the sanctuary for more than thirty-five years.

    Visit website

Northern Territory

3 places

  • Alice Springs Desert Park

    Arid-zone aviaries and a nocturnal house, plus a free-flight show whose Black-breasted Buzzard cracks eggs with stones.

    Visit website

  • Bowali Visitor Centre (Kakadu National Park)

    The main visitor centre for Kakadu National Park, whose wetlands hold vast flocks of Magpie Geese and over 280 recorded bird species.

    Visit website

  • Territory Wildlife Park

    A walk-through monsoon aviary and a free-flight birds-of-prey show, both built around Top End species.

    Visit website

Australian Capital Territory

5 places

  • Australian National Botanic Gardens

    Canberra's national collection of Australian native plants, with a rainforest gully that holds Superb Lyrebird and Gang-gang Cockatoos, the city's faunal emblem.

    Visit website

  • Jerrabomberra Wetlands

    Purpose-built hides overlook Latham's Snipe, crakes and the elusive Australasian Bittern. Managed by the Woodlands and Wetlands Trust.

    Visit website

  • Mulligans Flat Woodland Sanctuary

    Australia's largest single area of Box-Gum Grassy Woodland managed for conservation, in north-east Canberra, enclosed by a predator-proof fence. Birds it names among the species it protects are Bush Stone-curlew and Superb Parrot, alongside reintroduced Eastern Bettongs and Eastern Quolls.

    Visit website

  • National Zoo & Aquarium

    Combined zoo and aquarium on Lady Denman Drive, Canberra, built around a bookable Barking Owl encounter. Bush Stone-curlew, Superb Parrot, Eastern Whipbird and Little Penguin are also in the collection.

    Visit website

  • Tidbinbilla Nature Reserve

    Large reserve in the Tidbinbilla Valley spanning wetlands, grasslands and wet and dry forest. The Sanctuary is a predator-fenced wetland of interconnected ponds reached by a wheelchair-accessible path with boardwalk and viewing platforms, where waterbirds, platypus and turtles are among the residents; Emu and Superb Lyrebird are named elsewhere in the reserve.

    Visit website

North Island

18 places

  • Aroha Island Ecocentre

    Twelve-hectare predator-managed sanctuary in the Kerikeri Inlet, Bay of Islands, linked to the mainland by a causeway, with bush walks and an inlet beach. The site names North Island Brown Kiwi as its headline species, seen in natural habitat, alongside resident bush and shore birds.

    Visit website

  • Auckland Botanic Gardens

    Sixty-four hectares at Manurewa including around ten hectares of remnant alluvial forest of mature rimu, kahikatea, miro, mataī and tōtara, with a loop trail the Gardens promote for listening to native birdsong. The Gardens support ongoing bird monitoring in the forest.

    Visit website

  • Gannet Safaris Overland

    An overland four-wheel-drive route from Te Awanga through farmland and native bush to the clifftop gannet colony at Cape Kidnappers, described by the operator as the world's largest and most accessible, with over 20,000 gannets.

    Visit website

  • Kapiti Island Nature Tours

    Guided access to Kāpiti Island, one of New Zealand's most important nature reserves. The predator-free island is best known to birders for Little Spotted Kiwi, which overnight visitors go out to find after dark.

    Visit website

  • Kiwi North (Whangārei Museum & Kiwi House)

    Twenty-five hectares of farmland, forest and bush at Maunu with a purpose-built nocturnal house holding a pair of North Island Brown Kiwi in deep leaf litter, with a pond, simulated dawn and dusk, and burrow cameras for viewing when the birds are denned.

    Visit website

  • National Aquarium of New Zealand

    Marine Parade aquarium at Napier whose Penguin Cove exhibit houses Little Penguin (kororā) on a beach-and-pool habitat, and whose Native NZ Icons area displays kiwi alongside tuatara and tuna kuwharuwharu. It runs a Little Penguin rehabilitation centre and a kiwi breeding programme.

    Visit website

  • National Wetland Centre — Rotopiko

    Peat-lake reserve at Rotopiko / Lake Serpentine between Hamilton and Te Awamutu, with boardwalk, two short loop walks and a self-guided Discovery Trail through kahikatea forest and wetland margins. The National Wetland Trust promotes a mass fly-in of thousands of birds at dusk.

    Visit website

  • Ngā Manu Nature Reserve

    A 14-hectare reserve at Waikanae protecting one of the largest remnants of coastal lowland swamp forest on the Kāpiti Coast, with aviaries holding New Zealand Kaka, Kea, Red-crowned Parakeet (kākāriki) and Blue Duck (whio).

    Visit website

  • Ōtari-Wilton's Bush

    A hundred hectares of mature and regenerating podocarp and northern rātā forest and stream valleys a few kilometres from central Wellington, enclosing New Zealand's only public botanic garden dedicated solely to native plants. The Trust promotes the forest for its prolific native birdlife and birdsong.

    Visit website

  • Ōtorohanga Kiwi House

    A Waikato wildlife park built around North Island Brown Kiwi and its nocturnal kiwi house. A large dome aviary, walk-through enclosures, wetlands and forested trails also hold Kea and Blue Duck (whio).

    Visit website

  • Pūkaha National Wildlife Centre (Mount Bruce)

    A forest wildlife centre in the Wairarapa, known for Manukura the white kiwi and for its resident hand-reared North Island Kokako, Kahurangi.

    Visit website

  • Pūkorokoro Miranda Shorebird Centre

    The gateway to the Firth of Thames shellbanks and tidal flats, where Bar-tailed Godwit (kuaka) and Wrybill are the birds people come for.

    Visit website

  • Rotokare Scenic Reserve

    A predator-free sanctuary around a lake in Taranaki, and an active recovery site for Stitchbird (hihi).

    Visit website

  • Sanctuary Mountain Maungatautari

    A fenced maunga ecosanctuary in the Waikato, home to South Island Takahe and North Island Kokako. North Island Saddleback (tīeke) and North Island Robin (pītoitoi) can be met on the trails of Te Tūī a Tāne, the Southern Enclosure.

    Visit website

  • Tarapuruhi Bushy Park

    A predator-fenced forest sanctuary and historic homestead near Whanganui, with tracks through old-growth forest and Stitchbird feeders along the way.

    Visit website

  • Tiritiri Matangi

    An open island sanctuary in the Hauraki Gulf, and one of the most reliable places in the country for South Island Takahe (takahē), North Island Saddleback (tīeke), North Island Kokako and Stitchbird (hihi).

    Visit website

  • Wingspan National Bird of Prey Centre

    New Zealand's national bird of prey centre at Rotorua, built around flying displays of the New Zealand Falcon (kārearea).

    Visit website

  • Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne

    New Zealand's first fenced urban ecosanctuary, in the hills above Wellington, home to South Island Takahe, North Island Saddleback, Little Spotted Kiwi (kiwi pukupuku) and free-flying New Zealand Kaka.

    Visit website

South Island

12 places

  • Albatross Encounter Kaikōura

    Boat-based seabird watching off the Kaikōura coast, where great albatrosses and mollymawks come in alongside shearwaters, petrels, skuas and terns. The operator describes Kaikōura as holding one of the greatest varieties of seabirds in a small area anywhere on the New Zealand coast.

    Visit website

  • Brook Waimārama Sanctuary

    The South Island's largest fenced sanctuary, in the hills behind Nelson. Orange-fronted Parakeet (kākāriki karaka), New Zealand Kaka, Rifleman and South Island Robin live inside the fence, and there is a live kaka camera online.

    Visit website

  • Dunedin Botanic Garden

    New Zealand's first botanic garden, thirty hectares in North Dunedin, with a walk-through aviary on the upper-garden slopes holding native and overseas species and a co-ordinated captive breeding programme for New Zealand Kaka. Forty wild bird species have been recorded in the garden, among them New Zealand Bellbird (korimako), New Zealand Pigeon (kererū) and Tui.

    Visit website

  • Farewell Spit

    A nature reserve and wetland of international importance curving off the top of the South Island. The wader-watching area draws Bar-tailed Godwits, knots and turnstones, and there is an Australasian Gannet colony near the lighthouse. Public access beyond the first few kilometres is by supervised tour only.

    Visit website

  • Kiwi Park Queenstown

    Family-run wildlife sanctuary below the Skyline Gondola with purpose-built nocturnal kiwi houses holding Brown Kiwi, plus a conservation show and a self-guided trail through the aviaries. New Zealand Pigeon (kererū), Campbell Island Teal and Paradise Shelduck (pūtangitangi) are among the collection.

    Visit website

  • Natureland Wildlife Trust

    Small not-for-profit zoo on Tahunanui Beach Reserve, Nelson, whose collection includes Kea, New Zealand Kaka, Morepork (ruru) and New Zealand Pigeon (kererū). Its Conservation Corner is a breed-for-release aviary run with Project Janszoon and the Department of Conservation, releasing native birds into Abel Tasman National Park.

    Visit website

  • Orokonui Ecosanctuary

    The South Island's fenced cloud-forest sanctuary north of Dunedin, home to South Island Takahe, South Island Kaka and Southern Brown Kiwi (Haast tokoeka).

    Visit website

  • Rakiura National Park Visitor Centre

    The southernmost Department of Conservation visitor centre, at Oban on Stewart Island/Rakiura, with displays on the island's natural and cultural values and information on Ulva Island/Te Wharawhara, a predator-free open bird sanctuary.

    Visit website

  • Royal Albatross Centre (Taiaroa Head)

    Taiaroa Head, at the tip of the Otago Peninsula, holds the world's only mainland breeding colony of Northern Royal Albatross (toroa).

    Visit website

  • Te Anau Bird Sanctuary (Punanga Manu o Te Anau)

    A free, self-guided Department of Conservation sanctuary on the shore of Lake Te Anau, where South Island Takahe (takahē) are the birds most people come for, alongside Antipodes Island Parakeet and Brown Teal (pāteke).

    Visit website

  • Travis Wetland

    Urban freshwater wetland and nature heritage park in eastern Christchurch with raupō beds, open water, sand dunes and regenerating tōtara and mataī forest, served by boardwalks, a viewing tower and a bird hide. Australasian Swamphen (pūkeko), New Zealand Scaup (pāpango), Australasian Shoveler (kuruwhengi) and Sacred Kingfisher (kōtare) are among the residents, and Brown Teal (pāteke) have been released on site.

    Visit website

  • Willowbank Wildlife Reserve

    A wildlife reserve on the edge of Christchurch, family owned and operated by the Willis family for more than fifty years. It was the first in New Zealand to open a glass-free nocturnal kiwi house, so kiwi can be watched behaving naturally without a barrier.

    Visit website

Northeast

17 places

  • Audubon Society of Rhode Island Nature Center & Aquarium

    Coastal trails at Bristol with a boardwalk out over the Narragansett Bay marsh. The trails cross the 28-acre Claire D. McIntosh Wildlife Refuge, running from the pollinator garden through upland meadows to the marsh boardwalk.

    Visit website

  • Braddock Bay Bird Observatory

    A major banding station on the Lake Ontario shore, and one of the premier spring raptor migration sites in the east.

    Visit website

  • Cape May Bird Observatory

    The observatory at the tip of Cape May Point, where the peninsula funnels autumn hawk and songbird migration into one of the most concentrated movements in North America.

    Visit website

  • Connecticut Audubon Society Center at Fairfield (Birdcraft)

    Birdcraft, one of the first private songbird sanctuaries established in America. Five acres of mixed hardwood forest, thickets and a shallow pond, circled by a loop trail and boardwalk, with more than 150 species recorded.

    Visit website

  • Constitution Marsh Audubon Center & Sanctuary

    Audubon's tidal-marsh sanctuary on the Hudson River, among Marsh Wrens and herons. The 270-acre tidal wetland is an Audubon New York Important Bird Area and a New York State Bird Conservation Area.

    Visit website

  • Cornell Lab of Ornithology (Sapsucker Woods)

    The global hub of ornithology at Ithaca, with trails through Sapsucker Woods and the Lab's live feeder cams.

    Visit website

  • Drumlin Farm Wildlife Sanctuary

    A working farm and wildlife sanctuary with resident owls and hawks, plus Bobolinks in the grassland.

    Visit website

  • Edwin B. Forsythe National Wildlife Refuge

    The Wildlife Drive out over Barnegat Bay, good for Brant, herons and shorebirds. More than 48,000 acres of southern New Jersey coast with over 360 species recorded, and an eight-mile Wildlife Drive with two observation towers and a marsh boardwalk.
